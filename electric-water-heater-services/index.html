A standard electric tank has two elements and two thermostats that take turns instead of heating at the same time. The upper thermostat has priority: it heats the top of the tank first, then hands power down to the lower element, which does most of the day-to-day work. A high-limit switch (the red reset button above the upper thermostat) cuts power if the water ever overheats. That arrangement makes symptoms a real clue. No hot water at all usually points to the upper element, the upper thermostat, a tripped high-limit, or the breaker. Hot water that runs out quickly usually means the lower element or lower thermostat has quit. In St. Augustine, FL, we see all of these every week.

Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • A hissing or sizzling noise near the lower element
  • A circuit breaker for the heater that will not stay on
  • Hot water that runs out after just a few minutes
  • Dangerously hot water, a sign a thermostat may be stuck
  • Rusty water, leaks around an element, or water at the base
  • A red reset button that keeps tripping
  • Scorch marks, melted insulation, or a hot smell at the wiring
  • No hot water at all from any faucet

Why Hire a Professional for Electric Water Heater Services?

A typical electric water heater sits on a dedicated 240-volt, 30-amp circuit, which is reason enough not to learn on it. The thermostats sit right next to live terminals behind the access panel, and one slipped probe can start a dangerous arc. Replacing an element also means draining the tank, and forgetting to refill it completely means the new element burns out the moment power returns. Symptoms also overlap. A tripped reset can come from a stuck thermostat, a grounded element, or a loose connection overheating a terminal, so guessing often replaces the wrong part.

Professional service gets you the right fix the first time and a safe heater afterward. Rather than guess, we meter each element and thermostat, check for shorts to the tank, and inspect every wire and terminal for heat damage. Repairs are done with the tank drained and refilled correctly, and we verify amp draw once power is back on. When replacement makes more sense, we confirm the breaker size and wire gauge match the new heater and install the expansion tank, drain pan, or seismic straps your code requires. You end up with hot water you can count on.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here's where to begin:

  • Flush the tank regularly to keep minerals from piling up around the lower element
  • Keep the thermostats set around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
  • Lift the T&P valve lever yearly to make sure it opens and reseats
  • Protect your elements from hard water with a softener or upgraded low-watt-density elements
  • Never turn the breaker back on until the tank is completely full of water
  • Get a diagnosis for any reset button that trips more than once
  • Replace a worn anode rod before corrosion moves on to the tank itself
