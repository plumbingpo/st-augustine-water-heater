Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • No hot water at all, even after waiting for the tank to recover
  • A puddle under the tank that keeps growing
  • A T&P valve that releases water repeatedly instead of staying closed
  • A breaker that trips every time the electric heater tries to run
  • Black soot or heat discoloration around the burner door or draft hood
  • Any gas odor around the heater, which means getting out and calling the gas company before calling us
  • Violent rumbling or banging from the heater as it runs
  • Water at the tap that is suddenly scalding hot

The Benefits of Professional Emergency Water Heater Repair

Emergency calls bring together electricity, gas, heat, pressure, and water, which is exactly the mix that makes do-it-yourself work risky. An electric heater sits on a 240-volt circuit that should never be handled while standing in water. A gas heater has fuel piping, a burner, and a vent that can leak exhaust into the room if something is out of place. The tank itself holds scalding water under pressure, and a discharging relief valve is signaling an unsafe condition that must be fixed at the source, not muted. Capping that valve, relighting next to a gas odor, or forcing a rusted connection are all common and dangerous mistakes.

A trained technician brings the right order of operations and a truck full of the likely parts. Our team confirms the heater is isolated, tests for gas leaks with a detector and soap solution rather than guessing, checks household water pressure and thermal expansion when a relief valve has been discharging, and inspects the vent for proper draft before a gas unit is restarted. Equally important, we tell you honestly when a heater can be repaired and when it cannot. A leaking seam or rusted-through bottom cannot be fixed, while a failed valve, fitting, or element usually can, and the difference is often a few hundred dollars versus a new heater. You get that answer before any work begins.

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Add a battery leak alarm or an automatic shutoff valve that closes the supply when it senses water
  • Store chemicals and flammables far from the burner, and keep the area around the heater uncluttered
  • Test the T&P valve annually; a valve that sticks or weeps afterward should be replaced
  • Know where the heater shutoff and the main water valve are, and make sure everyone in the house does too
  • A drain pan with a proper drain line under the tank can turn a flood into a manageable puddle
  • Annual flushing keeps sediment from causing the rumbling and hot spots that lead to leaks
  • A 120F setting is safer for skin and easier on the heater than the hottest setting
