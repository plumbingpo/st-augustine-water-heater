Two quiet processes decide how long a water heater lasts: sediment and corrosion. Sediment comes from minerals that separate out as water is heated and sink to the bottom of the tank, where they cut efficiency, cause popping and rumbling on gas models, and overheat the steel or bury the lower element on electric ones. Corrosion is held back by the anode rod, a sacrificial rod of magnesium or aluminum that gives itself up so the tank lining is spared. Neither one is visible from the outside, and by the time a homeowner notices, the tank is often leaking. That is the reason maintenance matters for St. Augustine, FL homes: it interrupts both problems while they are still cheap to fix.

Here is what the visit covers. We flush the tank until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. For electric equipment, we meter the elements and check the thermostats and wiring. Then we verify the expansion tank still holds its air charge, confirm a safe temperature setting near 120 degrees, and look over every fitting for early signs of a leak.

Tankless water heaters have their own maintenance needs. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially in hard water, and eventually triggers error codes, reduced flow, or temperature swings. We flush the heat exchanger with a pump and descaling solution through the service valves, clean the inlet filter, check the venting and condensate line on condensing models, and review the unit's error history. Warning Signs to Watch For

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Small drips at the drain valve or connections
  • Shorter showers before the water turns lukewarm
  • A tank that pops and knocks during a heating cycle
  • Scale warnings or intermittent shutdowns on a tankless system
  • Higher gas or electric costs for the same routine
  • Hot water that looks cloudy or rust-colored
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water
  • No memory of the last time the water heater was serviced

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Set the water temperature around 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Flush annually, more often where the water is hard
  • Glance at the pan and connections regularly so small leaks are caught early
  • Plan yearly descaling for a tankless heater, sooner if scale warnings appear
  • Leave open space around the tank, especially near a gas burner
  • Treat hard water to cut down on sediment and scale
  • Ask about the anode rod at each visit; it is the tank's main defense against rust
