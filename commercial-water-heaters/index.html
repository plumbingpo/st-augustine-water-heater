How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Rust-colored water, leaks at fittings, or moisture under the heater
  • A heater old enough that a failure feels like a matter of time
  • Unsteady delivery temperatures that suggest a failing mixing valve
  • Distant fixtures that take minutes to run warm
  • Dish or hand sink temperatures drifting below what your inspector expects
  • Rising gas or electric bills with no change in business volume
  • Rumbling, popping, or banging from a heavily used tank
  • Lukewarm water at the exact time you need the most

Don't Wait for a Total Failure

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. If you notice any of these signs, contact St. Augustine Water Heater at (904) 335-6364 for a prompt inspection.

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

The scale of commercial equipment changes the job. Gas input is often several times that of a home unit, which affects gas line sizing, meter capacity, combustion air, and venting. Electric models can draw enough current to require serious service capacity. Storage often runs at 140 degrees or higher, so thermostatic mixing valves are needed to deliver safe water at fixtures, and those valves must be matched to the flow and tested. Recirculation loops need the right pump, working check valves, and balancing, or hot water short-circuits back to the heater and distant fixtures stay cold.

Code compliance and scheduling matter just as much. Permits and inspections are standard for commercial work, larger tanks may carry extra requirements, and a failed inspection can keep a kitchen or laundromat closed. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • Plan replacement before an aging unit fails during peak season
  • Keep recirculation pumps and check valves in service to avoid long waits and wasted energy
  • Test thermostatic mixing valves periodically to confirm safe delivery temperatures
  • Flush commercial tanks more often than residential ones, often every few months in heavy use or hard water
  • Keep the area around the heaters clear for combustion air and service access
  • Log water temperatures at key fixtures so drift is caught early
  • Check anodes on a schedule matched to your volume and water quality
