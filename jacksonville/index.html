How Jacksonville Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush every year if their water is hard.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or a damp spot under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored for a few years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and shorten the life of the tank, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number on your phone now so you can reach us fast as soon as something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

A water heater in Jacksonville almost never fails without warning. Pay attention to rumbling and popping during heating, brownish hot water, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, moisture around the base, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. These are early signs of sediment buildup, a spent anode rod, a failing element, or corrosion starting at the bottom seam, and caught early, they're usually an affordable repair or a planned replacement. Ignored, they lead to a failed tank and water on the floor. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it's the tank telling you pressure or temperature is running too high, and it needs a professional look soon. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so the small problem never gets the chance to grow.
