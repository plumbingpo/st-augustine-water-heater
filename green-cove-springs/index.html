How Green Cove Springs Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Many premature tank failures trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard so the heat exchanger stays clean.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the month and year it was built, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that range and also shows discolored water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water starts eating the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a surprise cleanup.
